# Lore figure library expansion — 7 October 2026

Added **1,102** figures to `Data/celebrities.json`. The library grows from **212 to 1,314**. Existing entries and their order are untouched; the new ones follow them in the file.

**973 of the additions are rated AA and 129 are rated A** by Astro-Databank. The rule is the one the library already had: a recorded birth time from a birth record (AA) or from the person, the family or a contemporary announcement (A), a named birthplace with coordinates, and a stated time standard. Nothing rated B, C, DD, X or XX was taken.

## How it was done

1. A list of about 2,500 well-known names across all fourteen categories was drawn up, leaving out everyone already in the library and everyone the earlier batches had rejected.
2. Each name was looked up on Astro-Databank on 7 October 2026 and the record read as published: date, clock time, place, coordinates, time standard, data source, Rodden rating and source notes.
3. Only AA and A records were kept. A-rated records were read one by one and dropped when the note described the time as approximate ("about", "around", "shortly after midnight"), rectified, inferred from an Ascendant, second-hand, or in conflict with another statement. AA records with an unresolved conflict in the notes were dropped as well.
4. Every kept record was checked against what is generally known of the person (year and place of birth) to catch namesakes. Astro-Databank returned the wrong person for, among others, Brian May, Sam Smith, Moby, Morrissey, Renaud, Tim Cook, Dick Clark, Fritz Lang, Michael Moore, Maggie Smith, George III, Prince Edward, Queen Anne, Giordano Bruno, Lorenzo de' Medici, Bonnie Parker, Henry James, Raphael, Beaumarchais and David Cameron; none of those went in. Records whose published place or coordinates looked wrong (Vincenzo Bellini, Pope Paul VI, Faustina Kowalska, Ferdinand von Zeppelin, the physicist Sadi Carnot) were left out too.
5. The library entry and the reference entry in `tests/Lore.Tests/Reference/adb-reference.json` were built from the same transcribed line, each line carrying a checksum made when the page was read, so a slip in copying a date, time or coordinate fails the build instead of reaching the file.
6. `FigureLibraryTests` then checked every figure: same date and clock time as the reference, and the same instant in UTC as Astro-Databank's stated time standard gives, to within a minute.

Dates are Gregorian throughout; where Astro-Databank gives an Old Style date it also gives the Gregorian one, and that is the one stored. Coordinates are Astro-Databank's, converted to decimal degrees. Place names were tidied for display (Paris arrondissements, hospital names and regional suffixes removed; English forms of city names) without touching the coordinates. Each bio is one neutral sentence.

## Clock standards that differ from the time-zone database (38 figures)

For these, the time-zone database Lore normally relies on says the clocks at the birthplace were set differently from what Astro-Databank records for that town on that day: mostly American towns before 1967, when daylight saving was a local matter, and places in wartime Europe. Astro-Databank researches these case by case, so its standard is used: the entry carries `utcOffsetFixed: true` and the offset Astro-Databank states. Lore then calculates exactly the instant Astro-Databank does. The Worksheet labels such an offset "set by hand", which is the wording written for a reader's own charts. If these are not wanted, they are the only bundled entries with `utcOffsetFixed`.
